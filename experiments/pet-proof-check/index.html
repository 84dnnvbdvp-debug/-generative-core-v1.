Pet Proof Check

Someone says they found your missing pet. This page will not decide whether they are a scammer. It helps choose the next independent verification step before money, codes, personal information, travel, or trust changes hands.

1. Who do they claim has your pet?
2. Is any of this happening before independent verification?

Two rules this tool intentionally does not use

It does not try to detect AI from a picture. Photos, videos, reverse-image searches, metadata, or provenance signals can contribute evidence, but none of them proves that the sender physically has your pet.

It does not say “never pay anything before the pet is released.” Real public shelters may charge legitimate reclaim, boarding, vaccination, or treatment fees before release. The safer boundary is: verify the institution independently, verify that it actually has your pet, then use its official payment/release path.

Sources and boundaries

This prototype is a bounded Vire Public Lab experiment. It stores nothing, sends nothing, and makes no automated fraud finding. Public visibility is not validation or endorsement.

If you believe a person actually stole your pet, is demanding ransom, is threatening you, or a handoff would be unsafe, preserve the messages and contact local law enforcement rather than trying to settle the interaction through this page. For immediate danger, use your local emergency service.
